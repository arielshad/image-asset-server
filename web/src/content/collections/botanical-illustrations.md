## Plants, drawn for science

Before photography, botanists relied on artists: Redouté's roses and lilies, the Curtis's Botanical Magazine plates, Japanese and Chinese flower paintings, and countless herbals with hand-coloured engravings. They are accurate, decorative and almost all in the public domain.

These pages collect botanical drawings and prints from the museums' open-access collections and from Wikimedia Commons, where libraries such as the Biodiversity Heritage Library have uploaded whole volumes.

## Using them

Botanical plates work for packaging, wedding stationery, wallpaper and textile patterns, wellness and food brands. Download the largest file for print and keep a note of the book or museum it came from: a credit such as *Pierre-Joseph Redouté, Les Roses, 1817* is appreciated even when it isn't required.
