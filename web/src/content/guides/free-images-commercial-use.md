## The short version

Every result on Image Asset Server carries a **verdict** that answers one question: can this go into something you publish commercially, such as an ad, a product page, a client website or a printed book?

| Verdict | Means | Typical licences |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial use OK | No conditions | CC0, public domain, museum open access, US government works |
| OK with credit | Use it and credit the source | CC BY, NASA media, MIT/Apache icon sets |
| Restricted | Conditions that usually rule out normal use | CC BY-SA |
| Needs a paid licence | Buy a licence or releases first | Shutterstock, Getty, Adobe Stock |
| Check the terms | Nobody has verified the rights | Archive items with no rights statement |
| Non-commercial only | Personal and educational use | CC BY-NC, CC BY-ND, personal-use licences |

Searches from AI agents are commercial-safe by default: only the first two rows come back unless the agent says the project is non-commercial.

## Where the licences come from

Wikimedia Commons, Openverse and Iconify record a licence per file or per icon set. Museums (the Met, the Art Institute of Chicago, the Cleveland Museum of Art) release their open-access images as CC0. NASA's media guidelines apply to everything from NASA. The Library of Congress gives a rights statement per item, and "no known restrictions" is read as free to use.

## What a licence doesn't cover

A licence covers the copyright in the picture. It does not give you the rights of the people in it, the trademarks on a shop sign or the design of a private building. For news, articles and education that is normal. For advertising, packaging or anything that suggests a person or brand endorses you, pick images without identifiable people and logos, or get releases. NASA images carry an editorial flag for this reason: NASA does not allow its insignia or its astronauts to be used in ads without permission.

## Free stock sites

Unsplash, Pexels and Pixabay give photos away under their own licences: commercial use is allowed, credit is optional, but you may not resell the photos unaltered or build a competing service from them. They can't be searched automatically here, so every search links to the same query on their sites.

Verdicts are a summary, not legal advice. Read the licence linked on each image before you publish something important.
