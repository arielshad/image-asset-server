## Three museums, one search

| Museum | Open-access images | Best for | Largest files |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Metropolitan Museum of Art | 400,000+ | Paintings, Japanese prints, armour, antiquities, early photography | Originals of several thousand pixels |
| Art Institute of Chicago | 60,000+ | Impressionism, American art, prints and drawings | Any width through IIIF |
| Cleveland Museum of Art | 30,000+ | High-resolution art for print | Full-resolution TIFF |

All three are searched at once, alongside Wikimedia Commons, which holds many more museum scans uploaded by volunteers and institutions.

## Using the files

- **Web:** a 1600–1920 px copy is plenty. The detail view's download buttons pick it for you.
- **Print:** use the original. At 300 dpi, an A3 poster needs about 3500 × 4960 pixels; Cleveland's TIFFs and the Met's originals often reach that.
- **Credit:** not required for CC0, but a caption such as *Claude Monet, Water Lilies, 1906. Art Institute of Chicago* is good practice.

## What to watch

Photographs of three-dimensional objects (sculpture, armour, ceramics) are also released, but their photographer's copyright is what the museum waives: it's the same CC0. Works by living or recently deceased artists stay in copyright and are not searched. When the museum's record names a lender or a copyright holder, the work is left out.
