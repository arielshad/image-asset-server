## What NASA allows

Works made by US government employees as part of their job are not protected by copyright in the US. NASA's media guidelines build on that: NASA images may be used for educational, informational and commercial purposes, and NASA asks to be credited.

## What NASA doesn't allow

- **The insignia and logotype.** The "meatball" and the "worm" are trademarks. Don't put them on products or use them as part of your branding.
- **Endorsement.** Don't suggest NASA approves your product, service or organisation.
- **People in ads.** Identifiable astronauts and NASA staff need NASA's permission before they appear in advertising.

## Partner images

Many space pictures are joint work. A credit line naming ESA, the Canadian Space Agency, a university or a company means the image may be copyrighted by them. Image Asset Server marks those **check the terms** instead of **OK with credit**. ESA images are often CC BY-SA or CC BY, with their own conditions.

## Picking a size

NASA keeps the original plus 1920, 1280 and 640 px copies of most images. For the web, download the 1920 px copy; for print, the original, which for Hubble and Webb releases can be many thousands of pixels across.
